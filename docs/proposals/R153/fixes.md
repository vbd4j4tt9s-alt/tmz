## 1. Bigger notification badges ("increase the size of the notification bubble")

Every badge is 1.5x its R151 size: the INDEX count 24 -> 36 px, the MENU "!" 20 -> 30, the DAILY button 20 -> 30, the tab dots (Index and Daily tabs) 14 -> 21. Same circle, white ring (1.5 / 2 -> 2 / 3 px), glass shine, pop and halo; the count text grows with it (max text size 62% of the diameter), "9+" still fits the disc.
* `NotifyBadge151`: `B.Grow = 1.5`, `B.Sizes = {Count 36, Alert 30, Daily 30, Dot 21}`, `B.Overhang = {Count 9, Alert 9, Daily -1, Dot -4}` (the overhang past the corner grows with the badge, except where the screen's top edge or the tab's edge is the limit), `B.Margin` 12 -> 16. The three callers (`ChestIndex.client`, `DailyRewardsClient.client`) read the sizes from the module; `HudLayout` keeps `NotifyBadge.Margin` (fallback 16) round each wheel button, so the buttons did not move.
* Why 16: the biggest badge reaches `B.Extent(36, 9)` = 15 px past its corner (ring, shadow, pop, halo); the wheel's `CanvasGroup` must hold that, or the circle is cut flat again (the R151 bug).
* Tests (R151 `run_badges.sh`, which carries the R153 checks): sizes exactly 1.5x, ring and text scale, "9+" fits, and for the 8 layouts (360 x 640 .. 1920 x 1080, phones portrait and landscape) every badge's WHOLE reach is on screen and not cut by any clipper: INDEX count, MENU alert, tab dot (and the 9 DAILY screens). 3 new mutants (sizes back to 24 / 20 / 14, the alert hung off the screen, the count hung past the margin).

## 2. The night refresh screen fits the rook gate (`RefreshBarrier`) and the gate's keys stand upright

Cause: the R152 gate is a gatehouse wall at y 44 - 58 with the 7 biome keys hanging down to y 42.5 in front of it, and pointed shoulders (the haunches, wedges from the towers) under it, but the barrier stayed the R122 wall (188 x 53 studs, centred on the track-start plane): taller than the opening, wider than it (into the tower shafts), its sign centred at 52% of that height, so the moon and the count sat behind the keys.
* The barrier is now the OPENING, as a polygon: between the tower shafts (`HubDecorKit151.Gate`: `TowerX - TowerD / 2`, minus 0.4 stud = |x| 91.4; the shafts' inner edge is 91.8), from `line.Y - 1` up to 1 stud under the lowest thing that hangs in it (`min(BeamY0, KeyY - KeySize / 2)` = 42.5 -> 41.5), and under the haunches' slope. Five parts: the centre slab `BiomeRefreshWall` (|x| <= 68.67, y 3.12 .. 41.5, carries the sign and the countdown labels) and, each side, a wing slab (68.67 .. 91.4, up to y 32.98) and a wedge on it whose slope is the haunch's underside (the same line, so the two meet without overlapping). Together they tile the opening exactly: no gap to see the track through, no overlap of any haunch. `RefreshBarrier.Opening(line)` reads the kit (`Gate.HaunchRise / HaunchRun / HaunchOuter` are new kit numbers `HubDecor151` builds the haunches from; same numbers as fallback when the kit is missing) and gives `Core` / `Edge` for the wings. Still opaque, colliding, in the same Z plane (-102 .. -98); the wings are children of `BiomeRefreshWall`, so `MovementGuard`'s raycast, `MapService`'s destroy and `TrackRefreshSky` see one barrier.
* The sign (moon, count, caption, 3 dots) is the R124 sign scaled by `Fit` (0.825) so the block fills 86% of the centre slab's height and is centred on it: digits 24.8 studs tall (were 30), caption 5.1, moon 23.7, |x| <= 44 (the slab reaches 68.67). Stars sit outside the sign block. The wings are plain unlit paper.
* No part of the barrier enters a haunch, the gatehouse wall, a key, the sign or a tower shaft (true outlines in the x / y plane: wedges as triangles; checked per pair); it only tucks its outer edge under the (non-colliding) stone steps of the tower bases, so no gap is left beside the shafts. No face of it lies within 0.05 stud of a same-facing overlapping face of the gate or the paving (the run-up lanes, streets) (0.28 stud is the closest); the R149 detector on the owner's place with the track closed: 0 counted, 0 tight, with the 5 parts (`run_barrier_zfight.sh`).
* Left on purpose: a 1-stud dark strip (the blackout cover) shows between the panel and the keys, and 0.4 stud beside each shaft.
* Tests: `test_barrier153.luau` (the REAL `HubDecor151.Apply` gate, 77 checks: the 5 parts tile the polygon with sampled coverage, no overlaps, planes, sign, kit numbers, the keys), `check_barrier_zfight.py`, R122 `wall_test.luau` updated, 10 mutants.

### The gate's keys face the hub and read upright (owner: "make sure that the forest jungle and so on tiles at the track gate are facing the right direction and upright")

Cause (confirmed on the owner's screenshot of R152): the key frame was `fromMatrix(pos, (-1,0,0), (0,0,-1), (0,-1,0))`. A part's Top-face SurfaceGui reads along the part's LookVector (canvas x = local -Z) and runs down along its RightVector (canvas y = local +X) (`KeyboardTrack` note, measured on the owner's screenshots). That frame's LookVector is world UP, so every legend read from the bottom to the top with its tops toward the viewer's left (emoji on the left, the speed on the right).
* Fix: `HubDecorKit151.KeyFrame(pos) = fromMatrix(pos, (0,-1,0), (0,0,-1), (1,0,0))` (used by `HubDecor151.buildGate`): LookVector = world -X (reading to the viewer's right: a viewer in the hub looks +Z, his right is -X), RightVector = world -Y (down the canvas is down), UpVector = world -Z (the top faces the hub), right-handed, not mirrored. Same position, size and order (Forest on the left, world +X, to Storm Peaks on the right), so nothing else moved; the legend lines are top to bottom emoji, name, speed.
* Test (in `test_barrier153.luau`): for every key, the canvas axes are computed from its CFrame with the KeyboardTrack convention and must be reading = world -X, down = world -Y, top = world -Z, `reading x down` = +Z (not mirrored); the keys step -24 studs from Forest to Storm; the legend is on the Top face with emoji above name above speed; 2 mutants (the R152 frame, a mirrored one). Nothing else assumed the old orientation (`HubLife151` only edits line 3's text and colour; `BeginnerTutorial` targets the boundary line, not the keys; the R151 hub tests read the legends by name).
* Preview: `fixes.png` draws the keys' legends through each key's real frame, R152 sideways next to R153 upright.

## 3. The whole belt moves, not just the arrows (`TreadmillFx`)

Cause (measured, `test_belt153.luau` MODE before on the R152 src): the pattern's scroll lived in `Controller:Animate`, which only runs when `Policy.Animate` is true (quality tier 2+, not FastMode, not Reduced Motion) AND the belt is within 90 studs and in view; the chevrons (`SpeedGainPopup`'s V134 block) move at every quality within 140 studs (90 in FastMode). `ClientFxBudget` drops to tier 1 after a few slow seconds (Studio), and from then on only the arrows moved. Also the glow / stream layers ran 1.6 / 2.4x the chevrons' speed. (Not the cause: the image route (generated / grid / pending / uploaded all scroll), the texture that is scrolled, the R152 write guards (none in this path).)
* The scroll has its own gate, the chevrons': near (`Look.Scroll.Range` 140, `RangeFast` 90), on screen, not with Reduced Motion (kept on purpose: a moving effect); quality and FastMode no longer stop it. One clock per belt (`record.Travel`, studs at 3.0 training / 1.3 idle, wrapped), every layer at rate 1 (one rigid belt), each offset = base + travel x rate wrapped at its own tile (no jump), written only when it changed, on every route that painted the image.
* Live overrides for Studio: the local player's attributes `TreadmillBeltAxis` ('U' / 'V') and `TreadmillBeltSign` (1 / -1) override `Look.Scroll.Axis` / `Sign` (read every 0.25 s). **Studio check:** the pattern must run with the arrows. If it slides sideways set `TreadmillBeltAxis = 'U'` (KeyboardTrack measured the Top-face frame of a SurfaceGui on the owner's screenshots: its x axis runs along Z; a Texture probably shares it); if it runs backwards set `TreadmillBeltSign = -1`. Tell the owner's result and the default in `TreadmillLook151.Scroll` follows.
* `SpeedGainPopup.client.lua` (the arrows) and `TreadmillAnimation.client.lua` are not touched for this.
* Tests: `test_belt153.luau` with the REAL `BiomeVisuals` (7 machines), `TreadmillFx.Start`, `TreadmillBeltArt151` and the chevrons' own code in one world: every layer of every machine travels what the chevrons travel (9.0 / 3.9 studs in 3 s) at tiers 1 / 2 / 3, training and idle; 36 studs over 6+ wraps with no jump (biggest step one tick), offsets inside the tile; pattern and chevrons move on exactly the same distances / FastMode; none with Reduced Motion; writes only on change; 4 routes; a rescan; the overrides; the numbers equal the chevrons' (static). R151 `test_treadmills151` follows (low quality / FastMode now scroll). 9 mutants.

## 4. Speed popups 2x ("numbers should also be bigger", then "2x bigger") (`SpeedPopupStyle`)

* Size: text 22 -> 44, bolt 20 -> 40, box 150 x 36 -> 300 x 72, bolt box 24 -> 48, gap 2 -> 4, outline 2.5 -> 5. The pop (.45 -> 1, back 2), the fling (.4 s, cubic, +-75 degrees, 44..104 px, 7 slots), the fade (.5 + .15 s), the life (.65 s), the 10 a second, the caps, the colours, the font, the bolt and the 1K / 1M text are the R151 numbers (`test_popups153.luau` compares 180,000 poses of 300 plans with the R152 module).
* A 2x fan (R151's fan at twice the size, so the popups pile up exactly as before: ~46% of a popup under others) is 324 px to each side and 248 px above the head: more than a phone has. `SpeedPopupStyle.FanScale(w, h)` gives the multipliers X, Y of the offsets for the screen (2 x 2 on a computer): a direction without room (above the head on a landscape phone, the sides of a portrait one) takes what fits (assuming the head `Fan.HeadY` = 36% down, a 12 px margin, the widest "+999.5K"), the other one up to 3 to keep the fan's area. `SpeedGainPopup.client.lua` applies it in 3 lines (pooled popup `FanX` / `FanY`, `apply()`); the pooled field is 960 x 760 (was 560 x 420) to hold the biggest fan.
* Phones (share of a "+72.5K" covered by other popups; R151 47% everywhere): landscape 844 x 390 -> fan 3.00 x 1.20, 50%; 932 x 430 46%; 740 x 360 55%; 667 x 375 57%; 568 x 320 78%. Portrait 390 x 844 -> fan 1.11 x 2.38, 58%; 430 x 932 55%; 375 x 667 76%; 320 x 568 89%. Computers: 47% (the same fan, 2x). On 12 phone and 5 computer screens the biggest popup stays inside the screen and never lies over the menu button, hotbar, jump button or thumb zones; the balances / status rows are touched by at most 5% of the popups on computers and landscape phones (0.3% at 844 x 390).
* **Known limit:** a portrait phone has no room for a 2x fan below the status box and beside the balances stack (top right, 70 - 210 px): the fan is tall, the right tips pass behind the balances (the popups are drawn under the HUD), and on 568 x 320 and narrow portrait phones the pile-up is up to 1.9x R151's (the test allows 2x there, 1.25x elsewhere). The owner's landscape phone is the good case.
* The R151 checks (`run_treadmills.sh`: SpeedPopupStyle changed only in FormatGain and the 1/5 s step, SpeedGainPopup identical to 19d05d4) were updated to allow exactly this (the three client lines, the sizes / field / fan constants and `FanScale`) and nothing else; the belt-arrows block of the popup script is still byte for byte frozen (`speed_popups_frozen.sha256`). 6 mutants in `mutate153.py`; the R151 33 popup mutants are all still killed.
