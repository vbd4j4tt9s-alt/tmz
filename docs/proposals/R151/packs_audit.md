# R151 pack models audit: dislocated parts, connection, "the same mesh and shape"

Owner (verbatim): *"look into refining and loosing the packs that we currently have; ensure there aren't any dislocated parts; the packs are all using the same mesh and shape and so on; make sure everything is connected for the packs"*.

Picture: `packs_audit.png` (every one of the 42 ordinary designs front and side, the Void / Mech / Verity packs, the fixed defects before -> after, defects circled). It is a **three.js preview, not a Studio screenshot**: the 115 pouch MeshParts of the templates are uploaded assets (MeshId only) whose vertices and printed vertex colours are **not in the place file and cannot be downloaded here**, so each is drawn as a stand-in rounded pouch of its Size box. Everything else (seal, tear strips, Void, Mech, Verity, pads) is drawn exactly as the real modules build it. The fixes change only the giant packs, the Void and the pads, so the other designs look the same before and after (the picture shows the fixed ones before -> after).

Everything below was run on the Roblox mock with the **real** modules / client scripts of this checkout and the **real** pack templates read out of the owner's place (`sapkeyver.rbxl`: 42 designs, 115 MeshParts, MeshIds, Sizes, PackLocalFrames). Nothing was run in Studio.

## 3. Not defects, but you should know

* **Tear strips and the tall designs (needs a look in Studio).** The plain seal sits at -1.12 and the 8 tear strips at 1.11 (pack units), flush with a standard pouch that spans -1.04 .. 1.02. **11 designs' pouch rises above 1.07** (Forest_05, Desert_05, Desert_06, Lava_03..06, Crystal_05, Crystal_06, Jungle_05, Jungle_06; top 1.11 .. 1.81), so their strips lie **inside the pouch's box**, not on its top edge (and 2 of them, Forest_05 / Desert_06, reach .06 .. .07 below the seal's top). If the pouch is solid there, the strips are simply hidden; they would show only through a Diamond coat's .10 transparency (a bar floating inside the glass pouch), and the opening animation tears from `TearLipY` = 1.11 for every design, i.e. inside the body of these 11. The box cannot tell which. Not moved (it would add a visible strip to 11 designs and move the opening's tear line); see section 6.
* **The Void's halo and debris float on purpose.** 23 halo segments (`EventHorizon*`) and 3 debris blocks are welded to the pack (they pass the connection checks) but hang .1 - .3 unit off the pouch and spin: the R122 "event horizon". Blue in the picture. Option: leave; or hang the ring on four spokes.
* **Layered print stacks (z-fighting by the R149 rule, not changed).** `tools/zfight.py` counts same-way faces up to .02 apart. The Void's nebula / singularity discs (.004 - .018 apart, 20 pairs) and the Mech's stepped reactor / piston print (.010 - .016 per step, 12 pairs) are in that band. They are shallow printed layers of owner-approved designs; flicker needs less than about .0003 stud at 50 studs on a 24-bit depth buffer (`D^2 / (near x 2^24)`), and a >= .02 spacing would thicken the print by about .04 stud and move every detail on top. Listed by `check_packs.py` (32 pairs), not counted, any coplanar pair or any pair outside those named layers still fails.
* **Mesh layers.** 54 pairs of MeshPart boxes in the Snow / Desert_06 / Lava_02 / Storm_04 / Storm_05 templates have coplanar or near-coplanar box faces (Storm_05 `ApprovedMesh02` / `ApprovedMesh03`: 0.0000 - .0008). They are the layered shells of one design (glass over pouch, neon over metal); two boxes' faces say nothing about two meshes' surfaces, so they are listed, not counted.
* **The pack hovers over its pad** by design: more than 0 and at most .6 unit between the pack's lowest point and the pad's top (`SeedPackVisuals.Platform`, checked for all 84 pads).
* **Weather traits add no part.** All 8 trait combinations leave the pack's parts untouched (48 packs compared); the trait is an attribute and a `GardenItemFX` tag on the root. The weather effect's size follows the pack's **size** (`ChaseService` / `ChestService` pass `PackSize`), not its biome / tier scale (.86 - 1.19) nor the tall / wide designs, so its rings are up to 19% smaller than a Storm Pack04 (pack scale 1.19) and well inside Lava_06 (3.07 studs wide). Option: pass `VisualScale`. Not changed (it changes how the effect looks).
* **The Mech pack's body shows Forest_01's print.** Its pouch is Forest_01 painted (231,237,239); a part's Color only multiplies the mesh's vertex colours (the R149 finding), so where the armour panels do not cover it the Forest_01 print shows through, lightly tinted. (The Void's (9,5,20) swallows it.) Not verifiable offline.
* **No "King" / "Crowncore" pack exists.** King is a seed rarity (the Crowncore Tree); the Void pack gives it. There is no pack model of that name to audit.

## 5. Options for the special packs (nothing changed)

**Verity: the standard pouch with a neutral copy? Option 3 was built (follow-up commit).** `VerityPouch151` reads the Storm_02 template from the live place (`SeedPackRenderer.GetGeometry`), loads each MeshPart's `MeshId` with `AssetService:CreateEditableMeshAsync`, sets every vertex colour to white, bakes a MeshPart and publishes the result as `ReplicatedStorage.VerityPouchTemplate151` (attributes `Finished` / `Ready` / `Failed` / `Reason` / `Key` / `VertexCount` / `BakeSeconds`); the EditableMesh is destroyed on every path. `VerityPackArt` then builds the pack with the plain pack's own builder (same pouch size, `PackLocalFrame`, pivot, Bounds, seal and tear strips), every part 255,255,0 SmoothPlastic, and the face as Decals on the pouch's Front and Back. Bake failed / denied / not matching / never ran, or a viewport that asks before the server finished: the R149 sachet. The text below is the analysis that led there. The R149 reason stands (the pouch's print is in its vertex colours; a part's Color only multiplies them). A neutral twin (white vertex colours, like `FruitMeshes149`'s `_Neutral`) needs the mesh's vertices. **The place file has none**: `SeedPackMeshAssets/Storm_02/ApprovedMesh01` is a MeshPart with a MeshId, a Size, a PackLocalFrame and an empty `PhysicalConfigData`; the vertices live on Roblox's servers and every request for them is refused from here. So the options are:

1. **Keep the sachet** (R149, owner-approved look: pure yellow, face plastered). Cheapest, fully tested.
2. **Studio-side dump, then the fruit route.** A Studio script (run by the owner, whose account owns the mesh) reads each pouch with `AssetService:CreateEditableMeshAsync(Content.fromAssetId(id))`, writes its vertices / faces / normals into data modules like `ApprovedPlantMeshData1..N`; at runtime `FruitMeshes149`-style code bakes an `EditableMesh` with every vertex colour set white (a `_Neutral` twin) and the part's Color becomes the only colour. Feasible in principle, **not testable here**; needs the EditableMesh API available to the experience; large data (42 meshes) unless only Storm_02 is dumped.
3. **The same at runtime** (`CreateEditableMeshAsync` on the live MeshId, white the colours, `CreateMeshPartAsync`), per server, falling back to the sachet. Needs the experience to be allowed to read that asset as an EditableMesh; unknown, **cannot be checked here**.
4. **A generated pouch** (a parametric pillow like the fruit lathes: no vertex data needed) at the template's footprint, white vertex colours: closer to the pouch than the sachet is, but still not the same mesh; not verifiable offline.
5. A Highlight fill (flat yellow whatever the vertex colours) is **not** an option: Highlights do not draw in ViewportFrames (hotbar / Bag / Index pictures) and are capped per client.

**Void:** it already uses the standard (Forest_01) pouch. Options: keep as is (the halo is the design); hang the halo on four spokes so it reads as attached; or restack the disc layers >= .02 apart (+.04 stud of print) to clear the R149 z-fighting rule.

**Mech:** it already uses the standard pouch. Option: a neutral twin (as above) so the body is exactly the silver-white and not Forest_01's print through a white tint; or leave it (the armour panels cover most of both faces).

**Ordinary designs, "all the same pouch":** the print is baked into 42 different meshes, so one shared pouch is **not possible without re-authoring art** (choose one mesh and every pack loses its print, or re-bake a print texture over one pouch in an external tool). What can be unified without art changes: the tier / biome scale (all packs 1.00, losing the size ladder), or only the 11 tall designs' strips / `TearLipY` once the Studio check (section 6) says where each pouch really ends.

## 6. What the box reading cannot see, and how to settle it in Studio

The audit reads each MeshPart as its Size box. A part "touches" the pouch if it touches that box, which is the most generous reading (the real surface lies inside the box; the Void's / Mech's print was fitted to it by hand). So a part could still hover over the real, rounded pouch at its corners, and a tear strip inside the box could still be hidden. In Studio (Command Bar, Edit mode, read-only; **not run**), for each template clone it to the workspace, set its MeshParts `CollisionFidelity = PreciseConvexDecomposition`, anchor them at their `PackLocalFrame`, and `workspace:Raycast` with an Include filter on the clone: (1) straight down the line x = 0, z = 0 from y = 3 to read each pouch's true top / bottom (strips at 1.02 - 1.20, seal at -1.20 .. -1.04); (2) along +Z from z = -3 at every Void / Mech front-detail centre to read the real gap to the surface. A gap above ~.03 stud on the 11 tall designs or the Void's corner runes is then a measured defect to fix with those numbers.

## 9. Chip-bag shape variations (third follow-up commit: one roll PER PACK)

Owner: "make pack shape variations in blender; the pack shape will still be a chip shape but will be slightly different", then "Packs can be implemented and every pack can spawn with either of those variations ... shapes for visual representation in the index can just use the default pack shape". Picture: `pack_shapes.png` (today's bag next to the six variations, front / side / three-quarter, a ghost of today behind each; Blender 4.5 / Cycles on the CPU; the stand-in bag is a parametric chip bag on the standard 1.97 x 2.06 x 1.0 footprint with a checker "print" on its original coordinates, so you see the print follow the deformation). `blender/pack_shapes.py` is the script (`bpyenv/bin/python docs/proposals/R151/blender/pack_shapes.py`).

**How it ships.** The prints of the 42 designs live in each pouch mesh's vertex colours, so a new uploaded mesh per shape would lose them. Each variation is therefore a smooth **field over the design's normalised box** (u, v, w = x, y, z of the pack-local box, -1..1): `u' = u * sx(u, v, w)`, `w' = w * sz(u, v, w)`, `v' = v`. The same maths is in Blender (`pack_shapes.py`) and in Luau (`PackShapes151.Field`); the script writes `tests/pack_shape_samples.luau` (1,386 lattice points) and the test compares the Luau field with it, so the two cannot drift apart. At run time `PackShapes151` loads a design's own mesh with `AssetService:CreateEditableMeshAsync`, moves every vertex with `SetPosition` (colours and UVs are never touched; the normals are turned with the field's Jacobian, best effort), bakes it with `CreateMeshPartAsync` and keeps the result in memory; the pack builders clone it exactly like the place's own.

| # | Variation | What it does (scales of the box half-size) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pillow | puffier: depth +5% at the belly, width -4% |
| 2 | Hourglass | slimmer waist: width -9% at v = 0, depth +3% |
| 3 | Pear | fuller at the bottom: width +4% at the bottom, -4% at the top |
| 4 | Top-heavy | the reverse of Pear |
| 5 | Shoulders | the corners below each crimp rounded off: width -8% at the corners |
| 6 | Flat | a flatter belly: depth -15%, width +2% |

Fit: y never moves (pivot and bottom line unchanged); the top and bottom 6% (the crimps, `|v| >= .94`, where the seal and the 8 tear strips sit) are not touched at all; nothing goes outside the design's box by more than 5% (the test measures every vertex of all 252 pairs). A part sits on its mesh's bounding-box centre, so when the field moves that centre (a small part off the box's middle) the part's `PackLocalFrame` moves with it and the vertices stay where the field put them. `Visuals.Bounds` still reads the place's own template and holds every shaped pouch to within 5%.

### The roll is per pack, for life

* **When.** A pack rolls one of the six variations **uniformly** the moment it is made: a track spawn (`ChestService:RefreshWorldPack`) and every pack that goes straight into a Bag (`PlayerDataService:AddChest`: bonus rolls, daily rewards, the mystery pedestal, owner commands, the starter pack; a gifted pack keeps the shape it has) and the Verity hand-in (`ConvertVoidPack`). No reason to weight it: the six are equally "a chip bag", none is rarer or worth more, and a pack's rarity is in its tier and coat, not its shape.
* **Stored.** As the attribute `PackShape` on the world pack's model and seed, and as an **optional field `PackShape` (1-6) of the item record**, next to `TestGrant`: written only when it is 1-6, read through `savedPackShape`, serialised by `SerializeSeedRecord`, decoded by `_decodeSavedSeedRecord` (saves, gifts), so **ProfileVersion stays 22** and an older server just drops the field. The tool of a Bag pack carries the same attribute for the hotbar / Bag pictures and the hand.
* **Old records** (everything saved before this) have no field: they show **variation 0, the default shape, today's pouch**. Why not a deterministic fallback from the record id: it would change the look of every pack a player already owns on the day of the update (and would put up to six times as many bakes in front of a full Bag's first open), for no gain; a pack is "rolled when it is made" and these were made before there was a roll. The default needs no bake, nothing to migrate or write back, and a later change of mind is one function (`savedPackShape`). Garbage in a save (0, 7, 2.5, "3", NaN, a table) is the default too.
* **Same shape everywhere for that pack.** The track pack carries `seed.PackShape`; a steal clones the whole seed (`ConcurrentKeeperService:_acceptSeed`), so the carry (`ChaseService._createCarriedChest`), a drop (the dropped pack and its model) and a pick-up of the drop build the same shape; banking keeps it (`AddChest` takes `chest.PackShape`, 0 = the default kept for life, and never rolls again); the record's tool, the pack in the hand (`_holdPack`), the opening copy (a clone of the held pack), the hotbar / Bag pictures (`ItemPictures`: the picture key carries the shape, so two shapes never share a picture, and the hotbar's stack key carries it too) and a gift all use the same stored number.
* **Default shape: ONE mechanism, the flag `DefaultPackShape`** (the Index PACKS tab merged earlier put it in: `SeedPackVisuals.Bag`'s `defaultShape` argument marks the bag, `ItemPictures` gives the picture its own `|Plain` look, `SeedPackRenderer` and `VerityPackArt` build the design's own mesh / the default neutral pouch and never ask `PackShapes151`). It is the same default as "no `PackShape`" (a pack with none, 0, or garbage), with one difference that matters: **the flag wins over a roll**, so a picture made from a pack's own attributes can never leak a shape. It is used by the shop / catalogue pictures that build a bag: the market stalls (`MarketLayout`) and the shop's Mech viewport (`PackViewport89`) pass `defaultShape`. The Index shows no pack picture any more (the PACKS tab was scrapped); a picture there would set `DefaultPackShape` on its proxy. The reward panels' proxy packs (the Verity quest dialog, the daily reward cards, the treadmill bonus cards), `ItemPictures.SampleKey`, `TitleScreen104` and the hub's decor pack pass no shape, which is the default too: they are not made from a pack, so no roll can reach them, and not flagging them keeps them on the hotbar's default look (one template; the R147 Verity dialog test requires exactly that). The Void, the Mech and every special pack have no variation at all. The server's neutral Verity pouch is the default shape, so `VerityPouch151.PlainState` is `Ready` from the server at once for a flagged Verity picture and nothing is baked; the client-side plain bake the Index merge added (`RequestPlain`) stays as the safety net for a server whose pouch carries a variation, and is quiet when the API is missing. **The mystery pedestal's locked silhouette** is the default plain pack shape on purpose: every locked pedestal looks the same, so the silhouette can never give a roll away (as it never gave the rarity away); its Ready pack is drawn in the roll the owner will be given and `_give` hands exactly that shape over (one roll per owner and pack, runtime only: if the owner rejoins before taking it, it is rolled again).
* **Verity follows the roll, safely.** The server's neutral (white vertex colour) Storm_02 pouch (`VerityPouch151`, replicated, baked at start) stays the **default shape** and the fallback; the shaped neutral pouch of a Verity pack's roll is one more (design, variation) pair, baked on each side like any (`neutral` pairs set every vertex white first, read back to be sure). So the Verity pack costs at most six small extra bakes (one design), nothing is replicated that was not, and anything that goes wrong (not baked, denied, over budget, off) is the default neutral pouch, and if the server's own neutral pouch is not there at all, the R149 sachet, exactly as before.

### Builds never yield; the fallback is stable

Packs are built inside the track refresh (which runs from a Heartbeat), the steal's carry slot ("no yields between checking and reserving") and the Bag's grant, none of which may wait, so **no build ever yields** (the first version of this, merged earlier, waited up to 20 s inside the builder; that is gone). A build takes the baked template of the pack's (design, variation) PAIR when it is there, else today's mesh, and asks for the bake. What keeps that from ever flickering between shapes:

* A **new** pack is *settled* against the bake (`PackShapes151.Settle`): it keeps its roll only when its pair is baked (the build asks for it first; the mock bakes at once, Roblox does not); else it keeps the **default shape for life** (stored 0, counted as `Demoted`), so a pack is the same shape on the track, carried, dropped, banked, in the hotbar and in the hand. To make that rare, `ChestService:SkinWorldSeeds` plans the **next** refresh (tiers, sizes, shapes) right after applying this one and asks for its pairs, so they are baked during the ~5 minutes before it. The first refresh of a fresh server has no earlier plan: its 35 packs are the default shape (a documented cost); every refresh after it is shaped.
* A pack that **already has** a roll (a record) is shown in the default shape only until its pair is baked, then in its shape, never back: the hand waits for it first (`_holdPack`, at most 2.5 s, *before* anything of the opening exists, with the checks run again after), so the pack in the hand is what its picture shows; a client's picture build errors "still loading" and is retried, so a picture is never drawn in the default shape and then again in another; the pedestal redraws once.
* A pair that **cannot** be baked (no permission, no vertices, the API missing, a size that is not the mesh's, a bake that never returns, over budget) is **sticky**: that pair is the default shape on that side until the owner switches shapes off and on, whatever happens later. Nothing leaks (`Mock.alive == 0`), the status folder says which pair and why, and there is **one warning per session, only for a real failure** (a denied asset, a bad mesh, a time-out ...): a world without the EditableMesh API at all (a test world, an old client) is an expected fallback and is silent (reason `EditableMesh is not available in this environment`; the same for the Verity pouch). This is what the older market suites (R135, R149) trip on if a pack build warns.

### Budget (per side)

Up to 42 designs x 6 variations (+ 6 neutral Verity pouches) are baked lazily, **one EditableMesh alive at a time** (destroyed right after its bake), in the order asked (an urgent request, a pack in a hand, jumps the queue). The cache is an **LRU over vertices**: above `MaxResidentVertices` (400,000, about 19 MB at the 48 bytes per vertex the status folder assumes) the least recently used pair that is not pinned and was not used in the last 30 s is evicted; the pairs of the packs on the track are **pinned** (held by their seed table until the slot is respawned) so a pack that is stolen, dropped and carried can always be rebuilt in its shape; a pair unused for 15 minutes goes too (a 60 s sweep); nothing is baked at `HardResidentVertices` (600,000, about 29 MB) unless something unpinned can go first, and a pair that does not fit after that fails for good (stable). A mesh over 60,000 vertices is not reshaped. Evicting a template frees nothing while a built pack or a picture still uses its mesh; that goes when they do. The numbers are in `PackShapes151.Config`.

**Realistic worst cases** (the pouch meshes' vertex counts are not in the place file; the figures below use 5,000 vertices per pair as a typical, 20,000 as a dense print, 48 bytes per vertex; the status folder reports the real `Pairs` / `Vertices` / `Megabytes` after a warm-up, which is the number to read in Studio):

| Case | Pairs wanted | At 5k vertices | At 20k vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full server, many packs on the tracks**: 35 track packs (35 pinned pairs, at most), up to ~30 packs in hands (30 more distinct pairs at most, unpinned), a few drops | up to ~70 | 350k vertices = **17 MB**, under the soft cap: nothing evicted | wants 1.4 M = 67 MB; capped at **29 MB (600k)**: the pairs past the cap are not baked, so those packs keep the default shape (a refresh sees the demotions in `Demoted`) |
| **A player with a full Bag** (1,000 packs: about 40-100 distinct looks, 160 picture templates at most, 60 views) | up to 100 pairs | 500k = **24 MB**, over the soft cap: idle pairs are evicted LRU; pictures already built keep their meshes (clones share the data) | capped at **29 MB** of cache (+ what live pictures hold); pairs past the cap are the default picture, cached and stable |
| **Cold start** (server start, nothing baked) | 35 | all 35 first packs are the default shape; the plan for refresh 1 bakes ~35 pairs in the first minutes (about 0.1 - 0.7 s each, one at a time, yielding every 1,500 vertices) | same, longer |
| **Memory of one bake** (transient) | 1 EditableMesh | ~5k x ~100 B = 0.5 MB | 2 MB |

Per side the ceiling is the hard cap (29 MB of cached meshes) plus the meshes still held by built pack models and pictures (a picture template of an evicted pair keeps its mesh until ItemPictures evicts the template; at most 160 templates). If 29 MB is too much for phones, lower `Config.HardResidentVertices` (or raise it); nothing else changes.

### Switches and the owner command

`PackShapes151.Config.Enabled` (false: every pack is shown in the default shape) and `Config.Force` (1-6: every NEW pack rolls that variation). `/test packshape`: no argument = status (the mode, pairs / MB / vertices baked, evicted, failed, how many new packs kept the default because their pair was not baked, how the rolls fell); `packshape <1-6>` = every **new** pack (a track spawn, a bonus / daily / mystery pack, a test pack) rolls that one (packs already made keep theirs); `packshape off` = every pack is **shown** in the default shape (a flagged shop / catalogue picture always is), the cache is dropped, packs keep their roll and show it again after `auto`; `packshape auto` = a uniform roll. The status folder `ReplicatedStorage.PackShapeTemplates151` replicates `Off` / `Force` and the numbers (`Ready`, `FailureCount`, `BakedCount`, `Requested`, `Pending`, `Pairs`, `Vertices`, `Megabytes`, `Evicted`, `Demoted`, `Mode`, `LastFailure`; a client's own carry a `Client` suffix); clients redraw their pictures when shapes are switched on or off. `/test pack` and `/test rarepacks` (which write their records by hand) roll like any pack.
